Conceptual Framework

The Three Pillars of Computation

What can computers compute, what are their unbreakable physical limits, and what can be computed within practical time bounds?

01

Automata & Formal Languages

Deterministic and nondeterministic finite automata (DFAs/NFAs), regular expressions, context-free grammars (CFGs), pushdown automata (PDAs), and the Pumping Lemma.

Modern Application

Compiler Lexers & LLM JSON Decoding: How Outlines and Guidance enforce guaranteed JSON schemas by masking next-token logits with finite-state and CFG transitions.

02

Computability Theory

The Turing Machine model, Church–Turing thesis, decidability, Cantor's diagonalization, the Halting Problem, mapping reductions (\(\le_{\mathrm{m}}\)), and Rice's Theorem.

Modern Application

Ethereum Gas & Antivirus Limits: Why smart contracts require execution metering (solving the Halting Problem) and why infallible static anti-malware is mathematically impossible.

03

Complexity Theory

Asymptotic time/space complexity, classes \(\mathbf{P}\) and \(\mathbf{NP}\), polynomial-time verifiers, Cook–Levin Theorem, 3SAT reductions, and \(\mathbf{NP}\)-completeness.

Modern Application

Post-Quantum Crypto & SMT Solvers: How industrial Z3 solvers verify million-variable boolean systems and why \(\mathbf{NP}\)-hardness underpins modern asymmetric encryption.

Proof Mastery

The Pumping Lemma as an Adversarial Game

How to write flawless proofs of non-regularity and non-context-freeness without falling into logical quantifier traps.

Step 1
Opponent's Move

The Opponent Picks \(p\)

The adversary chooses an arbitrary pumping length \(p \ge 1\). You do not know \(p\), so you must treat it as a fixed positive constant.

Step 2
Your Move

You Choose String \(s \in L\)

You select a specific, strategically chosen string \(s\) whose length \(|s| \ge p\) (e.g., \(s = 0^p 1^p\)). It must depend on \(p\).

Step 3
Opponent's Move

Opponent Splits \(s = xyz\)

The adversary partitions \(s\) subject to \(|y| > 0\) and \(|xy| \le p\). You must analyze all possible decompositions.

Step 4
Your Winning Move

You Choose Pump Power \(i\)

You choose an exponent \(i \ge 0\) (e.g., \(i = 0\) or \(i = 2\)) such that the pumped string \(xy^i z \notin L\). Contradiction achieved!

Evaluation

Assessment Breakdown & Academic Integrity

Rigorous continuous assessment combining homework, quizzes, hands-on lab projects, and formal examinations.

30%
Problem Sets (4)

4 biweekly homework sets (7.5% each). Formal proofs, automaton designs, and complexity reductions.

10%
Diagnostic Quizzes

2 short in-class quizzes (5% each) at Weeks 4 and 11 testing immediate conceptual definitions.

20%
Midterm Examination

Week 8 comprehensive examination covering Regular Languages, NFAs, and Context-Free Grammars.

10%
Applied Term Project

Independent visual TM simulation or modern case study reduction paper (demos in Week 14).

30%
Final Examination

Comprehensive end-of-semester exam covering the full breadth of computability and NP-completeness.

⚠️

Academic Integrity Policy on Generative AI

Students may use AI assistance for high-level conceptual questions and proof structure verification, but all submitted proofs, state transition tables, and reductions must represent the student's independent mathematical work. Direct copy-pasting of AI-generated answers or solution manuals violates GIMPA School of Technology academic integrity standards.
